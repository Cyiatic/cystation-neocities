480i Patches

Why 480i?

A 480i patch is the game-side link in a better picture chain: it gives the game a denser interlaced signal. From there, Analogue 3D can optionally change how that image is delivered and scaled before it reaches your display.

Start in the game

Game side · the patched ROM

Many N64 titles spend most of their time in 240p: one 240-line picture at a time. A 480i-patched ROM instead uses the console’s 480-line mode, sending odd and even lines as two fast fields.

Finish at the screen

On original hardware, that 480i signal can go straight to a CRT or scaler. On Analogue 3D, Progressive Output and Horizontal Upscaling are optional, separate stages, not substitutes for the patch itself.

  1. 480i-patched ROM (game side): creates the denser, interlaced picture at the source.
  2. Progressive Output (optional, Analogue 3D): a beta mode that delivers the full source image every frame.
  3. Horizontal Upscaling (optional, Analogue 3D): doubles 3D horizontal detail while 2D stays native.
  4. Your display (display side): choose the CRT, scaler or 4K mode that suits the game.

The patch is the game-side link; Analogue 3D’s options are display-side links. Compare them per game instead of treating the chain as a universal multiplier. Games that use field-rendered screens may still need individual testing.

What changes on screen

  • Finer vertical detail. A 480-line image gives a compatible game more room for details that stack upward: type, thin edges, distant scenery and busy menus can read with more definition.
  • Still an N64 signal. It stays in the console’s standard-definition world. On a CRT or a good scaler it is a native kind of output, not a filter laid over the game.
  • Tuned per game. No switch improves every N64 title. Each patch is its own piece of work, built around the way that game draws its picture.

The trade-off

Interlaced video is made from alternating fields. A good CRT or quality scaler can make that look great, but a display that handles 480i poorly can show flicker or motion artifacts. 480i on its own gives a game a better picture option; it doesn’t replace the original look, and it isn’t a performance boost.
